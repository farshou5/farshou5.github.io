Your 7:41, as I see it

I pulled the film off your phone and went through it shot by shot. This page is the whole video in words — every scene as it actually plays, every line of dialogue with the grammar fixed — and, under each scene, what changed between the document you planned it from and the film you actually made.

7:41 · 1024×576 · 24fps saved to phone Aug 9, 2026 11 scenes · 5 speaking characters + the machine made in Veo — ineligible for the festival, by design of the rules

How to read this page

Timecodes are from the file itself. WHAT I SEE is the frame-by-frame description. Dialogue is written the way it should read — cleaned English — and where the fix is more than a comma, the original wording sits under the line in small grey. The vs. your document notes compare the finished film against the planning doc you sent me (the Google Doc), because the gaps between the two turned out to be the most interesting thing on this page.

1 · The prison legend · cold open
0:00 – 0:08

WHAT I SEE. A grey concrete cell under two fluorescent tubes. Four men in prison fatigues — one seated on a cot, one at a steel table, two standing. A cloud of white gas pours from the ceiling vent between them; the standing man presses a hand to his face. It is staged like a documentary reconstruction: flat light, wide lens, nobody looks at camera.

0:00
Narrator
"Prisoners who were told they were being gassed with poison died from the power of fear alone — even though they were only breathing harmless air." as spoken: "Prisoners, after being told they were being gassed with poison, died from the power of fear alone, even though they were only breathing harmless air."
vs. your document: the doc credits this to a NEWS REPORTER; the film plays it as plain narration over the cell. These four men never appear again — which is why every later analysis, mine and the other two AIs', voted this opening out of the rebuild.

5 · The road — the bottle and the echo
3:12 – 4:32

WHAT I SEE. Your best sequence, and it's not close. Helmet POV from the motorcycle: sunlit downtown avenue, a red light, a blue convertible pulled up on the right with its driver's arm on the door. Moe's face sits in the round mirror, bottom of frame — the film's cleverest composition, keeping him present through a whole scene with zero lip-sync. A plastic bottle arcs out of the convertible and rolls to a stop in the lane. Glowing consequence-windows open over the road, edged in cyan and violet: inside one, the drain choked with trash under rain; inside another, the same driver trapped in his flooded car. A third window shows the rider far behind. Moe brakes, walks back — the camera goes objective for the first time as he crosses the empty crosswalk — picks the bottle up, bins it. The light turns green; he rides off, small in the wide frame.

9 · The life montage
6:20 – 6:52

WHAT I SEE. No dialogue, one caption. Moe at an office desk in a navy shirt, typing — steady, employed, changed. The proposal: golden-hour garden, one knee, ring box, Layla's hands to her mouth. The two of them carrying boxes into a bare sunlit apartment. A hospital room — Layla in a blue gown, Moe at the bedside holding her hand. Then a laptop with code on the screen and a baby in a pink hoodie reaching over the keyboard, captioned: "FIXING MY FATHER'S CODE WHILE HE'S SLEEPING." — followed by the same girl as a curly-haired toddler, climbing at the same laptop.

vs. your document: the doc's montage had a wedding hall and "father watching proudly" — neither is in the finished film, which is striking: the father silently disappears from the story here, with no goodbye shot, and the film never marks his death at all; the audience has to infer it at the cemetery. Also: the two laptop shots of the little girl are real phone footage of a real child — the one part of this film that can never be reused under the festival's likeness rules.

after the credits

What your document knew before any of us

Two ideas were already yours

The whole three-AI rebuild of this film stands on two ideas — and both are in your planning document, not ours. One: the grave speech about learning to see without the lenses, ending with the case placed on the grave — that is UNCOUNTED's climax, almost beat for beat. Two: the note about the AI replicating someone from the past so you could talk to them — that is UNCOUNTED's premise. The film you shot cut the first and gave the second eight seconds at a dinner table. The rebuild just took your two buried ideas and built the house on them.

Everything else on this page — the lines worth keeping, what each scene does, which shots are genuinely cinematic (the mirror on the motorcycle, the wet street, the walk away from the grave) — is already folded into the UNCOUNTED decision page, block by block, in English and Arabic.
