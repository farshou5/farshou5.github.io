01

Where we stand

The film as it exists today cannot be submitted

Not "weak" — ineligible. The rules require the film to be generated inside Higgsfield and finalised in Cinema Studio, carrying Higgsfield's watermark and packshot. Your cut carries the Neural Stain logo on every frame and was made in Veo. On top of that: 1024×576, 32 kbps audio, faces that change between shots, a typo burned into the VFX at 1:54 (BLOCKEL → BLOCKED), and 7:41 runtime.

Judges triage thousands of entries in the first 30–60 seconds. As-is: 0%.

Rebuilt properly — the honest number

Three independent reads: Codex 1–3%, Kimi 5–15%, me ~5%. Realistic target is the ten $10,000 honorable mentions. The $500k / $200k / $100k prizes are a lottery ticket with 14 winners worldwide — anyone telling you otherwise is selling something.

Cost of closing the gap between 0% and 5–15%: 15–19 focused working days. You have 20.

What's genuinely good — and stays

The story spine. All three reads put it above the median entry: a father who cannot reach his son with words, so he builds him truth instead; the good-deed timer; the bottle that echoes into a flood; "Beautiful, but not her"; the granddaughter fixing his code; the lens case left on the grave. The problem was never your story. It was everything around it — and one unfired gun, fixed below.

One festival rule that affects the family

No real person's face or voice may be used — including yours. Moe, Layla, Ghena and the Father stay AI-created characters named after your family, not trained on their photographs. Your family is in the story, not on the screen.

The people, and how each one changes

You asked for them by name. These are the names used in every line below.

MOE
The son. Thirty. Yours.
Good arc, but in the old cut he changes in a montage, automatically. Fix: he gets one on-screen failure — he accepts emotional suppression once, feels everything go flat, and revokes it frightened. That single beat is what makes his refusal at the grave earned instead of handed to him.
FATHER
Sixties. Never named on screen — he is "Dad".
Currently a flawless wise man who belittles his son and hands him thought-control technology with zero ethical weight. Fix: one crack. He deflects his own diagnosis with a joke ("Old hardware"), and when Moe asks why now, he doesn't answer. Loving, flawed, dying — not an oracle.
LAYLA
Moe's fiancée, then wife.
Currently a laughing reward: date, wedding, pregnancy, grave — no personality. Fix: she gets the joke (it's hers now, not his), and she gets the one moment that costs him something: she notices his eyes drifting mid-date and calls it. He takes the lenses out. All three reads called this the strongest available human moment in the film.
GHENA
The eldest. Four years old in the last act.
She stays on screen — she is structural, not decoration. The laptop beat ("fixing my father's code") is the legacy payoff, but it plays visually, with no subtitle gag; one subtitled line in an unsubtitled film reads as an error.
LANA
The second daughter — the pregnancy.
Recommendation: keep her as the bump, not a child. The bump at the grave says exactly what the film means — the family keeps growing past his father's death; Lana is the future he'll never meet but made possible. A fifth consistent character is also the hardest thing AI generation does. One optional line puts her name in the film: "Lana's kicking."
THE HONEY SELLER
The man at the market. Two lines.
In the old cut the lens states accusations as fact ("fake smile, diluted product") with no evidence, and he's contrasted against an "authentic" old woman — a bad look and accidentally an argument that algorithmic suspicion equals truth. Fix: the lens gives a probability, Moe asks "Do you know that?", the lens admits it's an inference, and Moe walks away politely. He's a man, not a villain.
LENS
The AI voice. The seventh character — and the antagonist.
It never had a want. Fix: it keeps offering to take pain away — anger on the road, panic in the kitchen, grief at the grave. Always helpful, never forced. "Only if you allow it." The film is the story of a man learning to say no to it.
THE PRISONERS — deleted
The cold open.
Gone, unanimously. A debunked urban legend told as fact in the first ten seconds, using anonymous men as disposable bodies, on characters who never return — and it burns your most valuable thirty seconds. Replaced by the flash-forward below.

The five decisions — my answers are already written in above

The script above assumes all five. Flipping any one of them is a small rewrite, not a restart — just say the number.

  1. Five acts with the grave cold-open. assumed yes
    If you'd rather open in the den like the original, we keep everything else and lose the flash-forward — but then the ending has no promise to pay off, and the first ten seconds are two men talking on a couch.
  2. The suppression-taste beat in Act 2. assumed yes
    This is the one I'd fight for. Without it, the refusal at the grave is a nice moment. With it, it's the film.
  3. Lana as the pregnancy only, plus the optional "Lana's kicking" line. assumed yes
    If you want her shown as a child, I'll write it — it costs a fifth consistent character (the hardest thing AI generation does) and flattens the ending into a family outro.
  4. "Old hardware" as the father's deflection. assumed yes
    Alternative if you want it quieter: he just closes his collar over the labels and says nothing.
  5. Title stays Neural Stain. assumed yes
    Logo moves off every frame and into the end packshot, which the rules allow.

06

How we actually build it

The iron rule

Generation happens through the browser, on your unlimited web plan only — the same way we did the Mom video. The CLI, the API and the new ChatGPT/Claude plugin all bill credits per generation even when Unlimited is on (Higgsfield's own help page: "Unlimited access applies only on higgsfield.ai"). That's what burned 7,475 credits overnight. It does not happen again. Nothing is generated until you approve this script.

Order of work

  • 1 · Soul IDs first — Moe, Father, Layla, Ghena. Four identities, trained and approved by you before a single shot. This is what fixes the face drift, and it's why the old cut's future-self horror didn't land.
  • 2 · Shot list — every shot with its written prompt, camera, length and audio, sent to you as one page before generation.
  • 3 · Generate + QC — I drive the browser, review every take frame by frame, and send you contact sheets to approve, exactly like the Mom video.
  • 4 · Audio — clean voice takes, score, and real sound design. The silence in Act 2 and the silence at the end only work if the rest of the track is built properly.
  • 5 · Cinema Studio — final assembly there, because the rules require it. Watermark and packshot on the way out.
  • 6 · Public post — from your account, before 3 Sep, 11:59 PM PT. I'll draft the caption.

What I need from you, and only you

  • GO / NO-GO, and any line numbers you want changed.
  • Soul ID approvals (4 faces).
  • Yes/no on takes from the contact sheets.
  • The final Cinema Studio clicks if browser automation hits a wall.
  • Posting from your own account.
